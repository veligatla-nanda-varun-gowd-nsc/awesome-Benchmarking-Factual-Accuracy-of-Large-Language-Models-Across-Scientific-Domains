## 🧠 Topic Overview
Evaluating the factual accuracy of Large Language Models (LLMs) within specialized scientific domains—such as molecular chemistry, clinical medicine, and structural physics—presents a critical bottleneck in modern artificial intelligence evaluation. While state-of-the-art language architectures display fluid text composition and broad generalized knowledge, they frequently exhibit domain-specific hallucinations, introduce logical gaps, and fail to maintain exact factual grounding when processing technical or low-resource literature. Traditional benchmarks often over-rely on generalized trivia or simplistic multiple-choice datasets, which fundamentally fail to capture the multi-step, relational reasoning processes required for genuine scientific verification.

Benchmarking factual accuracy across these deep academic boundaries requires https://github.com/veligatla-nanda-varun-gowd-nsc/awesome-Benchmarking-Factual-Accuracy-of-Large-Language-Models-Across-Scientific-Domains/tree/mainspecialized datasets that stress-test a model's underlying spatial, chemical, or biological logic. Key technical trajectories in this research focus on replacing superficial text-matching protocols (like BLEU or ROUGE metrics) with fine-grained atomic validation frameworks and knowledge-graph tripling verification pipelines. Tools such as FActScore decompose continuous textual claims into discrete statements to audit factual density against verified databases. Concurrently, evaluating how models handle contextual ambiguity and missing or contradictory source citations helps map out the boundaries of an LLM's reliability. By systematically analyzing the core failure points where model assumptions override ground-truth scientific facts, this domain builds safer, self-correcting generation pipelines necessary for advanced academic and enterprise research.
